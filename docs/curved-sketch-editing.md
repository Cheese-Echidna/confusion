# Curved sketch editing

Trim, Extend and Break operate on straight lines and counterclockwise circular arcs. Trim also converts a full circle to the surviving arc. Boundaries are finite lines, full circles and finite circular arcs, evaluated analytically; intersections on the continuation of a boundary arc do not count.

The desktop sketch tools use these same editing operations. Click the portion to remove with Trim, the nearest endpoint with Extend, or an interior point with Break. Trimming removes the interval bounded by the nearest intersections (or an existing endpoint); no intersection deletes the curve. Extending chooses the next valid boundary beyond the nearest endpoint. Break shares a split point between its two pieces.

This follows the interaction described in [Autodesk Fusion's Trim and Extend reference](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/ENU/Fusion-Sketch/files/SKT-TRIM-EXTEND.htm). A full circle with only one distinct intersection, including a single tangency, cannot define a removable interval and returns an error. Break requires an arc rather than an unsplit full circle. Ellipses and splines are not editing targets or analytic boundaries for these operations. Hover trim previews and drag-to-trim gestures are not implemented here.

Edits retain the first surviving curve's identity, sketch owner, construction status and original points where their endpoint role remains valid. Circular pieces share their original center and an equality relationship. Break preserves original line length dimensions as distances between original endpoints. Trim and Extend remove affected curve relationships whose endpoint interpretation changes, retaining support radius/diameter dimensions and line orientation relationships. Point constraints stay on their original points; these points remain available when referenced. Driving and driven constraints and dimension label positions are cleaned together. Unrelated geometry and constraints remain untouched. Detached original points are removed only when nothing references them.

Each public operation uses a candidate design, validates it and commits only on success, including calls targeting geometry in an inactive sketch. The original active sketch is restored. Existing extrusions whose boundaries become incomplete require reselection; the editor does not silently invent a new extrusion boundary. If validation would reject an edit, the design remains unchanged.

Regression coverage in `tests/curved_sketch_editing.rs` includes line/circle/arc boundaries, wrapping circle intervals, arc midpoint removal, tangency, nearest endpoint extension, stable IDs and constraints, inactive sketch ownership, save/reopen, solver degrees of freedom after arc Break, and extrusion of a semicircular closed profile after Trim and Break (when the kernel feature is enabled).
